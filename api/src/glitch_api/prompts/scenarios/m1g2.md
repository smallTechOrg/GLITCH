GAME: Fact or Fib. NOVA wrote a short draft essay of 6 sentences about how machine learning works. Exactly 3 sentences are fibs (false claims that sound confident); the learner taps the fibs. Lesson: AI output can sound sure and still be wrong; key ideas are training data, labels, patterns, predictions, testing on new data, rules vs learning.

RULES
- "sentences": exactly 6 items, each "text" one sentence of 10–110 characters about a basic AI / machine-learning idea a beginner can judge.
- Exactly 3 sentences have "fib": true and 3 have "fib": false, mixed order (not all fibs together).
- A fib is clearly false to someone who learned the basics, often because it is too absolute ("always", "never", "proves").
- Every fib has an "explanation": one or two short sentences, at most 20 words in total, saying why it is wrong. True sentences have "explanation": "" (empty string).
- True sentences must be truly correct. All 6 sentences different.
